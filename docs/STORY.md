# Story planet and arrival

Open `labs.html#story`. The lab generates the story planet on load: the pinned grid kernel at 16,000 points (71,314 cells), radius 753 m at 10 m per cell, with the defense map's lane density. Generation takes about four seconds on the Mac Mini; the HUD reports the time.

The clearing is at the north pole, 150 m in radius. Every cell inside is open floor. The lane mouths on its rim are found as runs of open cells just outside the radius; one mouth of at most two cells stays open, the rest are sealed to rock. The base frame is rotated so the open mouth lies on the frame's -Z axis, where the armored gate will seat.

The lattice stays natural by default (`terraces: false`); islands level their own plots with the slab's skirt. The concentric terrace profile remains available in `src/core/terrace-profile.js` when a plate design needs it.

**Arrival.** L lands the SH02 on the landing island, 60 m from the pole. The rocket is already burning and falling from 900 m when the 4 s orbit shot opens; the descent eases into touchdown at 12 s, legs deploying at 40 m, then the authored shock with dust and scorch, a 2 s settle, the top door, and Isao rising over seven seconds from deep inside the hull tube, past the rim, before drifting clear. The pole is kept for the Stalheart. K skips to the landed state, R resets the camera. Drag to orbit and wheel to zoom at any time; touching the camera leaves the rail. The timeline is `src/domain/landing-sequence.js` and is Node-tested; the clips are played as authored and never blended.

Ownership: `src/content/story-defaults.js` (recipe and tunables), `src/core/terrace-profile.js`, `src/domain/story-planet.js`, `src/domain/landing-sequence.js`, `src/fx/launch-plume.js`, `src/labs/story-planet-mesh.js`, `src/labs/story-landing.js`, `src/labs/story-tab.js`. No module imports the game controller. The SH02 is pinned in `docs/sh-rocket-assets.lock.json`; credits in ATTRIBUTIONS.md.

**On a phone.** Start the dev server with `node scripts/serve.mjs --host 0.0.0.0` and open `http://<mac-lan-ip>:8155/index.html?story=4#td` on the same Wi-Fi, or use the published site `https://kai-denrei.github.io/stalheart/index.html?story=4#td` once main has deployed.

Validation: `test/terrace-profile.mjs`, `test/story-planet.mjs`, `test/landing-sequence.mjs`; `node scripts/browser-test.mjs --story` drives the real page through rest, descent, touchdown, live play and skip with screenshots.

**Stages.** Digits 0 to 8 or the stage buttons show the base as it grows: 0 empty pole, 1 landing, 2 the landing island, 3 solar island and one Rotor, 4 the armored gate on the open mouth with six wall segments each side, 5 the HUGIN tower and catcher (booster hidden), 6 the Stalheart at the pole, 7 the tank bay behind it, 8 the assembly line and the single 18 m antenna on its radar island. `src/content/base-layout.js` holds the plots and stages; `src/domain/base-plan.js` turns them into placements, tested for overlap, monotonic stages, the gate on the mouth and corner sag under the slab's 1.2 m skirt. Every island and structure is tangent to the sphere at its own centre. `src/fx/story-base.js` draws the plan for both the lab and the game: one instanced slab per island with the 4 m grid drawn in the shader, instanced walls, the gate, and the landmark models with their authored cycles. O frames an overview.

**The story is the game's front door.** A bare `index.html` is the story world at stage 1; the legacy campaign, rescue and raid name themselves in the query (`classic=1`, `mission=`), as do the practice mode and the automated probes (`src/core/story-route.js`). The burger lists story, defend (the finished base at stage 8, skipping the opening), arrival (the lab playing the landing), classic, rescue and raid; leaving a mode drops its switches. The story world never fetches the legacy camp boxes, relay vault or portal rings. In the story, `?cine=1` opens the arrival lab rather than the legacy cold open. The legacy gantry heart and containers exist only in classic, rescue and raid.

**Play here.** P, or the button, opens the actual game on this planet through the deep link `index.html?story=N#td` (short for `world=story&stage=N&threat=0.35&heart=none&cine=0`), with the lab's current stage, so the game shows the space as it is: at stage 1 only the rocket stands on natural ground. `heart=none` keeps the game's own heart off the pole; the story base places the Terraformer 3000 there at stage 6. `world=story` routes the controller's map through `src/domain/world-recipe.js`, which returns the story planet with the heart at the pole cell and 4 m walls; `threat` multiplies wave size (0.1 to 4). The default world is unchanged. The game's own sector reveal, gate placement and camp still apply; islands, the story build stages and the encounter beats are not in the game yet.

**Story-mode rules in the game.** With `world=story` the controller starts clean past the landing: `heart=none` draws nothing at the pole, so the Terraformer 3000 placed by the story base is the Stalheart; no portals are seeded and the wave clock never arms; the camp containers, the station ring and the Terraformer's hull and container yard are skipped. Wall cells from the base plan are rock to the pathfinder and the tank; the gate's cell stays open and the gate plays its authored clip open when the tank comes within 22 m, closing again behind it. Enemies do not exist yet in this world, so "non-core enemies cannot damage walls" is a rule for the encounter sub-project, where the enemy path field will also exclude the gate cell while it is closed.

**Stages from the menu.** In the story, the burger shows a stage strip under the tab bar: 0 to 8 plus the arrival cinematic, each a click away, no URL editing.

**The three hulls are the three lives.** Stage 7 stands the kit's three-bay transport diorama (`mork_container_low_diorama.glb`, game tier, pinned in `docs/container-assets.lock.json`) on its own island behind the Stalheart, doors toward the gate: bay 01 sealed, bays 02 and 03 open with a MÖRK parked inside, the roll-out clip held at zero so every hull starts inside. In the game the bays are the berths (`plan.bays` from `src/domain/base-plan.js`: a point inside each bay and a straight two-cell run out of its doors, carried as unit-sphere points so the hull starts in the bay itself rather than at the nearest lattice centre). The first hull rolls out of 03 on the diorama's own 8 s `Tank_Roll_Out` clip (the game's deploy drives the clip's clock, the player's hull stays hidden until the clip ends where the run ends, and the camera watches from behind the bay over its roof so the blend into the driving view never swings through the hull), the next out of 02 on the game's own drive, the last opens 01: the parked hull in a bay is hidden the moment it is the one being driven, so the bays read the lives without a counter. The roll-out replays when the bays land after a fresh load, as long as the player has not driven yet. `defend` in the tab bar skips the story to the finished base (stage 8), where this roll-out is the opening. The sealed bay's doors and ramp swing open over 2.4 s on a clip authored from the model itself (each node turns to the pose of its twin on the open bay 02, `swingClip` in `src/fx/story-base.js`). The story hull is 10 m long (`STORY_SCALE.tankMetres`, the game's unit scale set from it) and the diorama, authored at the kit's 13.28 m MÖRK, is scaled to match. Before stage 7 the game's own invisible camp near the pole still supplies the berths.

**One muzzle for every mode.** The game now draws a sentry's muzzle flash from the package's muzzle recipe, tune and colours, exactly as the Sentry / Impact lab does, scaled from lab metres onto the sphere; the old dot burst that threw sparks three cells wide is gone. In the lab, round projectiles fly nose first and their calibre follows the tracer size squared, so a Rotor round is under half a Needle's.

**Rounds stop at rock.** A sentry round's range is capped at the first rock it would fly into (its own parapet excepted), and it lands with a ring and a spark burst there instead of passing through.

**Encounter, the opening beats.** Isao's faces play over the landing in the game too: "Rough landing!" angry, then "So much to build!" delighted, as briefs. In the lab one medium-close shot on the nose is held from the door onward: the door opens, Isao rises out angry, then turns delighted, with a hold on the smile; no zoom, and a comms line shows the words. After the Rotor stands on a gated world: a tremor contact pulses on the radar far out past the tunnel, with Isao's line; the ground opens twenty-eight lane hops out (breadth first over open ground outside the clearing, the farthest from the pole at that hop: 289 m from the gate on the real planet; a greedy walk used to give up nine hops out), far enough that investigating it later is a tank trip, with the breach effect and the game's own orbit shot held on the whole planet through the opening and the first fodder emerging (`STORY_BREACH`: six seconds past the breach animation, then a 1.8 s blend back); the first easy enemies emerge from it and walk up the lane; the Rotor holds fire on its own (story sentries have no auto-targeting until the override), so they pile up against the gate; only then does Isao say the sentries are not ready for auto-targeting and the manual override hands the Rotor over. On the fifth kill Isao notes they are talking to each other and starts a study of the pattern (a seed for a later beat on reverse-engineering the alien language with machine learning; visuals to come from the operator's synthetic-learningx58 CRT neural-net demos); on the tenth, that their biomass will do as a printing ingredient. Without a gate the override comes straight after the print.

**Encounter, beats one to three.** In the story world at stage 1 or later, `src/domain/story-beats.js` runs Isao's script: two and a half seconds after the landing it grants the Rotor's cost and places a real build order on the wall down the tunnel: the rock cell touching the lane cell two steps past the forward cell (`KIT.rotorSteps`), chosen to the side; the sentry stands on high ground the way every sentry will in this mode (there is no slab under it). Every tower in every mode now perches on its wall's edge: the mount slides from the cell centre toward the open cell it covers (the story names the lane; otherwise the upstream neighbour, where enemies first appear) until the far edge of its pedestal, measured from the model, meets the rock's edge, never over it, so the barrels look over the edge rather than into their own rock. The lab's static Rotor approximates this with `KIT.rotorEdge`, which the platform hook registers as a floor socket so the placement rule accepts it and the tower mounts at ground level. Isao spawns at the landing island, flies to the socket and prints the Rotor with the game's own travel-and-print loop. The static Rotor model is skipped in the game, and the game's own opening garrison is skipped too, so the wall Rotor is the first print. A second and a half after it stands, the game enters sentry control on that Rotor: a 3.2 s shot turns three quarters of the way round the tower and settles behind it looking down the lane, then the optic takes over. Moving and shooting are separate: on a computer a click locks the mouse and the mouse aims, Space fires; on the phone a drag aims, the tank pad's side zones turn the mount and its fire button holds the trigger. The Rotor's barrel cluster winds up while firing and down after. Views follow the tank's keys: 1 map, 2 PoV with the barrels in the bottom of the frame, 3 third person behind and above the turret. The aim ray always starts at the optic on the mount; only the camera eye moves. The practice chrome (weapon swaps, posts, bench) is hidden in the story. Once a gate stands (stage 4), phage walk up the lane from six cells out, one every 2.5 s, never more than eight alive and twenty in all; the closed gate's cell is a wall to them, so they pile up outside where the Rotor can reach them. Without a gate no fodder comes. The piloted sentry fires at 2.5x its auto cadence with 5x damage per round (`STORY_PILOT`), so a phage drops in two hits, and the Rotor's tracer was promoted through the package path to a 6 px head with seven ghost points (`rotor-tracer-dense`). No field manual and no tutorial run in the story world; both will be rebuilt once the pieces exist. Isao's briefs still carry the old fiction and are the next text to rewrite for the new world.

**The planet is baked.** Relaxing 16,000 points for 80 iterations and carving 384 rooms took about 5 s in Chrome on every load (9 s in Node), by far the heaviest part of opening the story. `npm run bake` runs the pinned kernel once and writes `assets/story/planet.bin` (1.07 MB: the relaxed vertices as float32, the carve's tags, the heart, spawn, room seeds and distances, behind a header naming the recipe). `src/main.js` fetches it before the story routes start and `buildStoryPlanet` reads the vertices and the carve back instead of running them, rebuilding only the lattice and the cell graph (0.7 s in Chrome, 0.8 s in Node). A bake for another recipe, a missing file or a corrupt one is ignored and the planet is built live, so a stale bake can never change the world; `test/planet-bake.mjs` fails until the bake matches a fresh build again, so re-run `npm run bake` after changing `STORY_RECIPE` or the kernel. The rest of the story's weight was models: the solar complex alone was 9.2 MB, the Terraformer 4.1 MB and the HUGIN pad 3.2 MB even at their game tiers, 27 MB in all at stage 7.

**Models ship packed, landmarks in two tiers.** `npm run build` runs every `assets/models/*.glb` through gltfpack (meshopt compression with quantised attributes, named nodes, materials and extras kept, so authored pivots and clips still bind); the pinned sources are untouched and only the release copy is packed. Every GLTF loader carries the vendored meshopt decoder. Result: 89 MB of models become 14.9 MB in dist (the solar complex 333 KB, the diorama 189 KB, the MÖRK 202 KB); the whole release is 29 MB. Packing drops degenerate triangles, so the release acceptance allows the MÖRK's count within 1 %. On top of that the four big landmarks carry a far tier. The solar complex uses the owner's authored export set (`docs/solar-lod-assets.lock.json`: LOD1 game at 3,856 triangles and 7 draws as the near tier, LOD2 distance at 2,752 triangles and 1 draw as the far tier; the 131k-triangle detailed export is no longer loaded by the story). For the HUGIN pad, Stalheart and assembly line, `npm run tiers` derives `assets/models/far/*.glb` with gltfpack's simplifier at a tenth of the triangles (`KIT.lod.ratio`), pinned against their sources in `docs/far-tier-assets.lock.json`, until authored tiers arrive. `docs/ASSET-COLLABORATION.md` is the export contract and budget sheet for the assets manager. The story base loads the far tier first and stands on it; the near tier is fetched only once the camera comes within 150 m (`KIT.lod.metres`) and the two swap by camera distance with 1.3x hysteresis, so from the map and the orbit the whole base is a few thousand triangles and a landmark's full detail is never downloaded unless you go there. The upstream d1 to d3 files are damage states, not detail tiers (the Terraformer d3 has 39.6k triangles against the intact 40.5k), which is why the far tiers are derived rather than pinned. The story world at stage 7 now downloads about 3 MB of models on Pages instead of 27 MB, and draws the base at a few thousand triangles from the map.

**Red to white hot.** The Rotor's barrels carry their heat in every mode (`src/content/sentry-heat.js`, `src/core/heat.js`, `src/fx/barrel-heat.js`): every round adds heat, the barrels radiate it away faster the hotter they are, and the 'Edge' meshes under the ROTOR pivot glow from dull red through orange to white. At full heat the mount locks out (the optic reads OVERHEATED with the percentage) until it has cooled back under 35 %. Against the measured piloted cadence of about fifteen rounds a second that is about ten seconds flat out and four and a half to come back; an auto Rotor settles at a dull red and never locks.

**The first wave down is the next unlock.** Once the twenty fodder are spent and none stand, the beats enter `cleared`: Isao's line, and a view strip appears under the tab bar (`src/fx/story-views.js`): TANK leaves the optic and hands the hull back (`leavePilot`), SENTRY takes the printed Rotor again without the shot, MAP is the global view (the optic's map while piloting, orbit otherwise). Sentries still hold their fire on their own; auto-targeting is a later beat.

**The Quiver.** Four seconds after the wave is cleared Isao introduces the Quiver ("solid cores are coming") and prints it on the rock beside the lane one step nearer the gate, on the Rotor's side (the plan's second socket, `cells.quiver`, `KIT.quiverSteps`; the rock across the lane turned out to have no sightline to the gate pile). Once it stands one hard-cored enemy (a barbed, hp 3) emerges from the same breach; when it reaches the gate Isao says its targeting chips are not ready either and the override hands over the Quiver's optic with the Rotor as the second post. A second hard core follows nine seconds later, so the two can be locked and shot one after the other: hold the reticle on it until the lock takes, then fire. The story's Quiver fires the lab's TALON (`STORY_QUIVER.missile`: the game's quiver config with mesh talon, profile heavy, six seconds of flight, a 25x payload so one round takes one solid core) from its own pool, one round in flight at a time (the launcher holds until its guided round has landed, so the second hard core gets its own shot), with a first-encounter lock of 0.6 s inside a 4 degree cone and a 42 m reach, and while a TALON flies a spotting monitor (`src/fx/story-monitor.js`) renders the scene a second time, scissored into a small box bottom right, from a camera riding just behind the round. Both down: "two for two", the views unlock again and the beats sit at `settled`. While the lock is being tuned the hard cores hold off the wall: once inside a holding ring of lane cells two to four hops outside the forward cell (`STORY_QUIVER.hold`, `story.ring`) they only wander within it, within the Quiver's reach but never at the gate, and the override triggers at five cells from the gate rather than two. The strip names the mounts: TANK, one button per printed sentry (ROTOR, QUIVER), MAP; a mount button takes that optic, or picks it while already piloting (the pilot host's `pick`). Every mount sights from its perch at the lane edge, not from its rock's centre: a ray from the centre down the lane grazes the next ridge cell and reads as blocked, which is how the Quiver across the lane and then beside it saw nothing until this.

**The hull keeps off the sinkhole and out of the walls.** Every open crater (the breach module lists them with their radius) is a no-go for the tank: the free-move entry test refuses it, the cushion pushes radially off the rim the way it does off the Stalheart's pad, and the autopilot's exits skip cells on it. Enemies still emerge from it. The cushion also knows the hull has a nose and a tail: each end is nudged by the rock it is in or beside, with a band of the cell's edge plus the hull's half width, so a corner pushes the whole hull out while a straight lane leaves it be.

**Earlier landings.** Three HUGIN boosters stand out past the clearing from the landing on (`docs/hugin-flight-assets.lock.json`): two on their tripods, one wreck on its side (`tilt` and `lift` on the structure), each snapped to the nearest open cell to its frame point (`anchor: 'open'`), with derived far tiers so the map stays cheap. Draft placement until the tank trip beat is written.

**A day on the planet.** The story world has a sun (`src/core/daylight.js`, `src/fx/daylight.js`, `STORY_DAY`): a five-minute period, three of them daylight at the pole, the orbit leaning 60 degrees off the pole so noon stands 60 degrees up and the base gets a real night. Phase 0 is dawn; the night half of the circle turns a little faster so the day share holds. The light rig blends between the look's own night values (whatever the look just applied) and a day set that is the lab's old permanent rig (warm sun 1.6, pale hemisphere 1.5, a deep-blue background), the stars fade to 15 percent by day, and dawn and dusk ramp over about half a minute so nothing snaps. No shadows: three colours and two intensities a frame. The lab runs the same cycle from mid-morning and exposes `daylight()` and `setDay(phase)`; the game's acceptance state carries the phase.

**Build tag.** Top right, every page shows `build <token>` (the file hash the release build stamps into the page) or `dev` on the source tree, so a screenshot says which build it came from.

**Z-fighting.** Structures ride 6 cm above their slab and the slab material carries a polygon offset, so coplanar floors (the assembly line's plate, the solar generator's base) no longer flicker against the slab.

**Kit.** Base pieces come from the upstream base kit: the game-ready base kit (walls D0-D3, corner, vehicle gate, 4 m tile) and the 28-triangle scalable island slab are pinned in `docs/base-kit-assets.lock.json` under `assets/models/kit/`.

**Sound.** The owner-provided rocket thrust sample loops under the descent, scaled by plume intensity, and cuts at touchdown. The tank's spool-up plays for leg deployment and the door, spool-down for the landing. Cues are pinned in `docs/rocket-audio.lock.json`. The armored gate plays karyoku's hydraulics when it starts opening and its slam when it has closed (`docs/story-audio.lock.json`), in the lab and in the game. Six plume quads hang under the skirt, each breathing on its own cadence.
